# Time Machine Workshop: Facilitation Guide

## Overview

Two 60-minute sessions take eight people from wallet history to two or three opportunities worth starting now. Session 1 builds two opposing futures; session 2 walks back from them to today.

| | Session 1: Forward jump | Session 2: Return trip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mode | Diverge | Converge |
| Teams | Two teams of four, one world each | Teams swap worlds |
| Work | Build a 2040, 2035 and 2030 strip | Backcast, test a wildcard, vote |
| Output | Two world strips with signals | Scored opportunities and 2-3 committed moves with owners |

**Method.** Teams start at 2040, then move to 2035 and 2030. Starting far out stops people from extrapolating today. Session 2 then asks what had to be true at each step back, which turns visions into specific moves.

**Roles.** Eight participants in two teams of four. You facilitate, keep time and hold the default-drift baseline. You do not build a world.

**Materials.**

- Sticky notes in four colors, one per lens
- 30 dot stickers, or 3 votes per person on a digital board
- Printed lens cards and world briefs, one set per team
- The wallet evolution timeline as a pre-read
- A timer everyone can see

## The worlds

Two axes: who acts (a person directly, or an agent they delegate to) and where trust lives (with the user, or with an institution).

| | User-held trust | Institution-held trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agent-delegated** | **Team A: agents under my rules.** Personal agents act under rules the user writes and owns. | **Wildcard: institutional agents.** Agents act for you inside bank and brokerage rails. Revealed in session 2. |
| **Human-direct** | **Default drift (baseline).** Today's wallet, improved step by step. | **Team B: institution wallet.** The wallet dissolves into institutional apps. Custody is invisible. |

Teams A and B are built by the teams. The default-drift box is your baseline, and the wildcard is revealed in session 2. Print the table below as the world brief for each team.

| World | Core assumption | Push on this | Seed signals today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Team A: agents under my rules | Users keep their keys and delegate to agents through policies they write | Who audits what agents do, and who is liable? | ERC-7715 permission grants; MetaMask Agent Wallet limits and Guard Mode; Coinbase agent wallets with session caps |
| Team B: institution wallet | Institutions hold custody and users rarely sign | What does self-custody still mean, and who wants it? | Stripe owns Privy and Fireblocks owns Dynamic; US spot Bitcoin ETFs hold about $104B; OCC GENIUS Act rules exempt self-custody software from custody rules |
| Default drift (baseline) | Wallets improve step by step | Which problems are still unsolved in 2040? | EIP-7702 upgrades; passkeys; clear signing (ERC-7730) |
| Wildcard (session 2) | Agents act inside institutional rails | Which of our opportunities survive here? | x402 agent payments passed 138M transactions on Base by Q3 2026; GENIUS Act takes effect January 18, 2027 |

Seed signals come from the wallet evolution research report in this folder. Confirm dated figures before the session.

## Preparation

Send the pre-read two days before session 1, fix team and lens assignments in advance, and set up both boards before people arrive.

**Pre-read (send 2 days before).**

- The timeline of wallet milestones, one page
- The five unsolved problems today: recovery, signing comprehension, supply chain, delegation sprawl and cross-chain complexity
- One question to think about: which wallet change of the last 15 years did nobody see coming?

**Teams and lenses.** Assign these before the session so no time is lost. Mix functions across the two teams where you can. Inside each team of four, give each person one lens: the person, the attacker or regulator, the builder, the business. People keep their lens in both sessions.

**Room and boards.**

- One board area per team, laid out as in Board layouts
- A shared wall for the cross-world map in session 2
- Your own board for the default-drift baseline: today's wallet extended to 2030, 2035 and 2040

## Session 1: Forward jump (60 min)

Session 1 takes each team from a blank board to a three-column world strip, starting at 2040, and ends with each team pitching its world to the other.

| Minutes | Block | What happens | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-5 | Frame | Goal, ground rules, default-drift baseline | Shared starting point |
| 5-15 | Past and present | Five inflection points, five open problems | A calibrated sense of how often we are wrong |
| 15-50 | Build the worlds | 2040 (15 min), 2035 (10), 2030 (10) | One world strip per team |
| 50-60 | Swap pitch | 3-minute pitch each, then notes on the other team's world | Signals and "what would break this" notes |

### Block 1: Frame (0-5)

- Say: "We are not predicting the future. We are building two futures to find moves worth making now. Today we go to 2040. Next time we walk back."
- Ground rules: be specific, not clever; no world is right; the attacker and business lenses are allowed to be unpleasant.
- Show the baseline: today's wallet, improved step by step. Tell teams their worlds must differ from it in structure, not polish.

### Block 2: Past and present (5-15)

- Walk five inflection points, one minute each: HD wallets and seed phrases (2012-14), hardware wallets (2014), MetaMask and WalletConnect (2016-18), smart accounts and passkeys (2023-25), Bybit and agent wallets (2025-26).
- Present each as the problem that moved, not the technology: "don't lose the file," then "write down 12 words," then "understand what you are signing," then "decide what this account, app or agent may do."
- Name the five open problems today in three minutes: recovery, signing comprehension, supply chain, delegation sprawl, cross-chain complexity.
- Ask: "In 2012, which of these would this room have predicted?" Expect almost none. Say: "Ours will be wrong too. The value is in the reasoning."

### Block 3: Build the worlds (15-50)

Hand out world briefs and lens cards, then send teams to their boards. Each team fills one column at a time, using the rows on the board.

| Column | Time | Prompts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2040 | 15 min | Write the headline. Finish "In 2040 a wallet is ___." Name two things that no longer exist. Which UX pillar breaks first? |
| 2035 | 10 min | Same rows, shorter. Which shift between 2035 and 2040 made 2040 possible? Note it on a sticky marked T. |
| 2030 | 10 min | Same rows, shorter. What is already visible today that leads here? Name one thing that goes wrong. |

Facilitator moves:

- Call time at minute 30 and minute 40, hard. Say: "2030 will feel easy. That is the point: the near term is where concrete moves live."
- Around minute 25, ask each attacker lens: "How does this world get robbed?" Ask each business lens: "Who pays?"
- If a team drifts toward today, ask: "What is gone?"

### Block 4: Swap pitch (50-60)

- Each team pitches its world in 3 minutes: headline, "the wallet is...", and what is gone.
- Each team then spends 2 minutes on the other team's board, adding signals seen today to the signals row and notes on what would break the world.

### Outputs

- Two world strips, 2040 to 2030
- A signals row on each board
- Tipping-point notes between columns

## Between sessions

Allow two to five days between sessions. Teams add real-world evidence to their worlds, and you build the cross-world map that opens session 2.

| Who | Task | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each participant | Add three signals to the team board: something seen today that points toward the world (a launch, a rule, a paper) | 20 min |
| Each participant | Add one note on what would break the world | 5 min |
| Facilitator | Photograph or export both boards after session 1 | 10 min |
| Facilitator | Build the cross-world map from both boards | 45 min |
| Facilitator | Prepare the wildcard card and print the scoring template | 15 min |
| Facilitator | Send the recap and the map one day before session 2 | 10 min |

Look for three things when you build the map:

- Needs that appear in both worlds (for example, a signing or recovery need)
- Contradictions, where one world needs what the other forbids
- Gaps that neither team touched

## Session 2: Return trip (60 min)

Session 2 swaps the worlds, walks backward from 2040 to today, tests the results against a wildcard world, and ends with two or three committed moves.

| Minutes | Block | What happens | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-5 | Recap | Show the cross-world map and what overlaps | Shared starting point |
| 5-30 | Backcast | Teams swap worlds and walk back from 2040 to today | Enablers, dependencies, tipping points, opportunities |
| 30-40 | Wildcard | Both teams test their opportunities against a merged world | Survivors and casualties |
| 40-52 | Harvest | Silent dot vote, cluster, sort | Clusters labeled no-regret, bet or hedge |
| 52-60 | Commit | Score the top clusters, choose two or three, assign owners | Committed moves |

### Block 1: Recap (0-5)

- Show the cross-world map. Name the needs both worlds share and the contradictions.
- Say: "Today we walk backward. For each world, what had to be true to get there, and what would we do first?"
- Teams swap boards. Each person keeps their lens.

### Block 2: Backcast (5-30)

Teams walk their new world in three steps. Capture each answer on a sticky note marked E (enabler), D (dependency), T (tipping point) or O (opportunity), in the band under the column.

| Step | Time | Question | Note type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2040 to 2035 | 8 min | For 2040 to exist, what had to be true by 2035? | E, D, T |
| 2035 to 2030 | 8 min | For 2035 to exist, what had to be true by 2030? | E, D, T |
| 2030 to now | 9 min | What would we ship, test or decide in the next 12 months? | O |

Lens moves:

- The attacker lens challenges every enabler: "What would stop this?"
- The business lens asks of every opportunity: "Who pays, and why us?"
- The builder lens marks each opportunity as ready, hard or unknown.
- The person lens asks of each step: "Would I trust this?"

### Block 3: Wildcard (30-40)

Bring both teams to the shared wall and reveal the wildcard world: agents act inside institutional rails. Ask: "What if the two worlds merge?" Then go through the O notes one by one and ask: "Does this survive in the merged world?" Mark survivors with a star.

### Block 4: Harvest (40-52)

- Silent dot vote on the O notes: 3 minutes. Rules are in Scoring and voting.
- Cluster the top-voted notes: 5 minutes.
- Sort each cluster as no-regret, bet or hedge: 4 minutes.

### Block 5: Commit (52-60)

- Score the top five clusters with the template: 4 minutes.
- Choose two or three. Each gets an owner, a first step within 30 days and one thing we will not do.
- Say: "We check progress at 30 days."

### Outputs

- Two backcast boards
- A ranked, labeled list of opportunity clusters
- Two or three committed moves, each with an owner and a first step

## Lens cards

Print one set per team, one card per person. Each lens has its own sticky-note color, so the boards show whose view an idea comes from.

### The person (yellow stickies)

Your job: speak for someone living in this world.

- What do I do on a normal Tuesday?
- What do I no longer have to know or decide?
- What would make me stop trusting this wallet?
- Which UX pillar am I relying on most?

### The attacker or regulator (pink stickies)

Your job: find what breaks and what gets blocked.

- How would I steal from this world?
- What would a regulator require or ban?
- Which signing, recovery or permission step is weakest?
- What does the news story about a failure say?

### The builder (blue stickies)

Your job: say what we would have to build, and in what order.

- What would we have to ship by 2030?
- What is hard or still unknown technically?
- What could we prototype this quarter?
- What would this change in how we design today?

### The business (green stickies)

Your job: say who makes money and who loses.

- Who pays for this, and why?
- Who loses revenue or control?
- What would the company need to believe?
- What is our right to play here?

### Pillar reference

Use these five pillars for the "first pillar to break" row on the board: Trust Through Transparency, Continuous Feedback and Clarity, Risk Prevention and Recovery, Efficiency and Cost Respect, Adaptive Guidance.

## Scoring and voting

Three dots each give 24 votes. The no-regret test then separates moves that hold in every world from bets on a single world.

**Dot voting rules.**

- Each person gets 3 dots, placed only on O notes
- Stacking dots on one note is allowed
- Vote in silence for 3 minutes, before any discussion
- Starred notes, the ones that survived the wildcard, stay visible while people vote

**No-regret test.** Sort each cluster of top-voted notes:

| Type | Test | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-regret move | Appears in both team worlds and survives the wildcard | Start now |
| Bet | Appears in one world only | Run a small, cheap experiment |
| Hedge | Protects us if a world we did not expect arrives | Keep a watch item and a ready plan |

**Scoring template.** Fill it in for the top five clusters during the commit block. Time to start is now, within 6 months, or later. Choose two or three: prefer no-regret moves, add one bet if the team has capacity, and name one hedge to watch.

## Facilitator notes

Futures workshops usually go wrong in five ways. Each has a sign to watch for and a fix.

| Risk | Sign | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teams extrapolate today | The 2040 column looks like a nicer version of the current wallet | Ask "What is gone?" and compare with the default-drift baseline |
| One lens dominates | The builder talks most while the attacker stays quiet | Rotate who speaks first in each column; the attacker speaks first on enablers |
| Optimism bias | Nothing goes wrong in any column | Each team writes one failure per column |
| 2040 overruns | 2035 and 2030 get rushed | Call time hard at minutes 30 and 40; 2035 and 2030 are shorter by design |
| The loudest idea wins | Dots cluster right after someone's pitch | Vote in silence, before discussion |

**If attendance changes.**

- 6 or 7 people: keep two teams (3+3 or 4+3). In the smaller team, one person holds both the builder and business lenses.
- Fewer than 6: run one team in one world, use the default-drift baseline as the contrast, and skip the swap.

**If the board is digital.** Make each cell a frame, set one sticky color per lens, and lock the column headers.

**Closing checklist.**

- [ ] Pre-read sent 2 days before session 1
- [ ] Teams and lenses confirmed
- [ ] Boards, stickies, dots and printed cards ready
- [ ] Session 2 date set before session 1 ends
- [ ] Both boards photographed after each session
- [ ] Recap and committed moves sent within 48 hours of session 2
- [ ] 30-day check-in scheduled
