Wallet Time Machine · Session 1 · Incidents, not milestones

04 / 31

One line per incident, then move on. Mt. Gox: an exchange failed, and “not your keys, not your coins” became the rule for a decade. FTX: the same lesson, this time for the mainstream. Bybit: the keys were safe, but the screen showed a routine transfer and the signers approved a payload they couldn’t read. Binance, October 29, 2026: about $6.3B drained from hot wallets on ten chains, the largest crypto theft on record by TRM Labs’ count, which TRM attributes to North Korea’s TraderTraitor unit. On Ethereum it ran through EIP-7702, the 2025 milestone on the last slide, signed by Binance’s own hot wallet. Binance said it had covered 90% of the stolen amount from its own funds. The point to land: incidents aren’t milestones, they show where design failed, and the signing system keeps being the target. If someone later writes an incident on a milestone sticky, ask what shift it would push, and write that instead.
